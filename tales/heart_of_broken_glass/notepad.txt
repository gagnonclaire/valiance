
Trades instructors were a strange replacement for elder mystics the she was once taught by. Work as a technician, though, wasn't so different from the role she would have otherwise taken on were she not so callously uprooted so many years ago. She was one of many that reached into the abyss, willing it to serve. But the arcane machinery did not serve her in return. It churned away endlessly, a single cell of a largely replaceable organ. It gave life, in some small part, to the great bestial machine that was Alburn. An indifferent construct that neither cared nor recognized her.

A low buzz sounded from below, almost inaudible beneath the rumbling advance of the metro. A worn coupling, probably, burning what would otherwise be forward momentum off as waste heat. Only a small loss of efficiency, sure, but it should probably be replaced. It's unlikely that it would be.

The buzzing halted along with the car. Not Ceta's stop, but she got off anyways. She couldn't stand another moment of the incessant sound. She walked the rest of the way to work. This would make her late, but the world would continue moving in her absence, as it always has.

Nobody seemed to notice her late arrival, or at least kept it to themselves as she changed into her jumpsuit and eyed today's work orders. Regular maintenance on lines three and sixteen, already being handled by two other's. Nothing else, save a safety class towards the end of the day. In response to another accident on the manufacturing floor, most likely. They were all too common here, an obligatory sacrifice in a routine attempt to satiate the ceaseless hunger of the machine.

Refracted lights glittered along the streets below. Ceta followed them from left to right, right to left, back and forth. Her mind mirrored the movements, meandering through well worn corridors. Faded regrets, uncertain futures, melted into idle ambiguity. In rhythm she touched each finger of her right hand to her thumb, from index to pinky and back again. A familiar, but mostly purposeless ritual. A fidget to keep her body distracted while her mind wandered.

Heavy footfalls sounded above. An upstairs neighbor arriving home from work, evidence that the world still carried on. They nudged their way into Ceta's trance. Not breaking it, but bending it enough to redirect. Memories of Elizabeth stumbling lazily about the apartment. She could never speak of why work kept her so late. Ceta broke rhythm, had closing as the headlights below blurred out of focus. This wasn't a hallway she was prepared to go down. She pushed herself up from the windowsill and pulled the panes closed.

The muted rainfall substituted the usual musical ambience that otherwise filled the studio both night and day. She needed something to focus on, to put keep her mind leashed. Discarded equipment in bad need of servicing strewn across the coffee table. A half disassembled device glowing faintly on a crowded desk, waiting for long overdue repairs. Dirty dishes in the sink. But nothing seemed worth her attention. She inevitably found herself on lying on the couch, an obvious trap. Resigned, she closed her eyes and let herself drift. Maybe to better places. But more probably not.

The flashing lights of the city below briefly illuminated the details of a dimly lit office. A faded family photo on the desk, facing away from the man sitting at it. A gaudy certificate embellished with gold leaf hanging over his head. Bookshelves full of dusty, unlabeled books flanking him on either side. With a sigh and a hollow tone, he cut between her glances.

"I take it the decor is to your liking?"

The remark warranted no response. He looked past her towards the open door. She shifted slightly, centering herself back in his view.

"Ok... If you can take a seat and we can-"
"I'll stand."

She crossed her arms and fixed her gaze. The skyline reflected off of the razor hanging at her hip, now exposed. Standard pattern, but clearly made from something other than simple surplus steel.

"Ok, that's uh, fine."

He straightened his sleeves and gathered a small stack of papers.

"It should all be here, I can make a copy after-"
"I'll just take those."

A pause. He made a scene of searching his desk for something he could not find. When he surfaced, he was met with an expectant, outstretched hand. Old scars dug deep into the palm, and he followed them up a ravaged wrist until a snap pulled him back.

"Documents."

The man gathered himself and his papers, sleaves wrinkling around his arms. Standing from his desk, handed over the packet.

"If you need me, call-"
"I won't"

A sigh of relief was held back as to not betray any fear as the woman flipped through the folder idly. Her eyes stared through the pages until she raised them to meet a nervous gaze.

"And if you need me,"

She paused, a slight smile flashing teeth.

"Call me."

With a careless twirl she exited, accompanied by one last metallic shimmer. As his guest disappeared into the dark halls beyond the open door, the man slumped back into his seat. A shaky hand grabbed at a note on the corner of his desk. Scanning through it again and again, he found himself with the same smile. There would undoubtedly be blood, and more than he had paid for.

The comfortable repetition of a constant rumble of heavy tires was broken by a crackling voice.

"Five minutes out, get your shit together."

In the back of the armored truck, four operatives gathered their equipment. The first operative, a woman with a gravely voice, spoke over the sounds of shuffling cartridges and tightening straps.

"Anyone else feel like something's off here? Like there's something strange about this one?"

"No shit Syb, every job we do is so far beyond strange. That's like our whole thing. What's strange is you just now coming to that realization, what's strange is -"

"No need for that, let her explain."

The quiet interruption from the third operative's soft voice stilled the mounting tension, and the group leaned in unison as the truck took a sharp turn to the left. The soft rumbling of pavement gave way to rough country roads.

"One minute."

The tires ground against the gravel, the new soundscape resurrecting the sense of unease.

"Ok so what about you Jay, any thoughts on this? You think this one's a bit strange, maybe even weird? Maybe, maybe even a little -"

The truck came to an abrupt stop, and the fourth operative spoke plainly.

"I think we're here."

Four figures crept like shadows through the humid summer night along an unkempt trail. At the end of the overgrown path sat a small house, old but cared for, much unlike the road that led to it.

Among the trees, the sounds and lights of the city no more than a few miles north were all but nonexistent.

A man stood amongst the corpses. his face mostly obscured by an odd mask. A featureless circle, black as void. He did not turn to face her, even as she drew her razor. From his coat he drew his weapon, slowly, as if to savor the occasion. The pistol seemed to be made of the same material as his mask, featureless and dark. he pulled the slide halfway back, and glanced toward the glow emanating from within. with a quick release, it clicked back into place.

The muted rumbling of boots on pavement reverberated through the worn metal structure. Another herd to the slaughter. The man spoke as he raised his weapon towards the closed gate.

"What strange allegiances you have made"

Ceta held her tongue and watched as his hand traced slowly to his left, tracking something she could not see. A deft pull of the trigger sent the gun screaming through the heavy night. The shot found its mark in an instant, ripping first through the heavy gate, and then flesh. Two response team members, stacked one behind the other in preparation, reduced to a red haze. Inside of the old warehouse, the backblast summoned a storm of dust. Ceta, unshaken, set her gaze towards the man's silhouette. Turned towards her now, he again raised his weapon.

"But worry not, Daughter, for such things can be unmade."

the second shot, true as the first, met the angle of her blade and swung skyward, leaving a moonbeam in its trail. She was left staggered, for a moment. Her razor weas left cracked, and her skin sheared from her left forearm. Before the third shot could be sighted, she made her move. Two fast dashes about the center line between them were all it took. A lethal follow through with her razor, flung a black spray across the floor. A slight twitch on the edge of the man's mouth, right at the mask's event horizon, was the only reaction.

More quickly this time, he lined up his shot. She once again brought her blade to meet the bullet. But the angle was too sharp, the metal too stressed. Steel splinters cut through her with explosive force, throwing her to her back. Dust still lingering, she could see only the black of his boots as he advanced. She willed her shredded sinew to stand, and he stopped. Still some distance from her, his short path was marked by a trail of blackened blood. With a casual curiosity, he once again checked the chamber of his pistol. He seemed uninterested in her struggle, unbothered by the gash in his chest, and unamused as she raised a defiant shard to him.

A breaching charge sounded from behind him, a facsimile of the blasts that have already sundered the warehouse. Tracers flew through the dark, both combatants caught in the suppressive fire. With every bullet through her, Ceta's heart quickened. She watched as the man turned and raised his arm to block his face. Her ears rang, but her vision sharpened. She watched as the man began to raise his weapon towards the intruders, but caught a glint of metal to her side.

In one motion, Ceta retrieved the hilt of her blade with her left hand and plunged the broken edge into her right. As she pulled it from her wound, a fresh scarlet edge emerged. Back towards the gunfire, a dark figure, strobed against muzzle flashes, approaches her. One flick of her blooded blade cuts the figure in two, the force of the motion creating a gap in the cloud of debris. On the ground before her lay two halves of a response team member.

The dust settles to a momentary silence. Ceta grips a bloodstained hilt in her left hand as her vision blurs. She sees something moving towards her, but can't make out the shapes. She hears a familiar call, but can't place the voice. On her hand and knees, in a puddle of her own blood, she sucks in a series of excruciating breaths. She feels agony fade to numbness, then a sharp jab to the side of her neck brings the world back into focus. Sputtering blood, she tries to speak.

"Ca- Cali-?"
"Stop struggling. You bisected your medic so we're on borrowed time here."

Calliope gives a string of order to her one remaining team member. He's standing over the top half of the medic, digging into bloody pockets while speaking into a radio.

"I- sor-"
"Don't wanna hear it right now."

With medical equipment retrieved, Calliope and her last remaining team member get to plugging holes as sirens approach.
