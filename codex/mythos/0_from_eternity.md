# From Eternity

The world, as it stood eternal, was perfectly stable machine. Reality rained from the heavens, was filtered through sea, decayed in the abyss, and was finally exhausted through the core at the bottom of reality. This gradient from order to chaos defined this world, unchanging process stretching back forever.

This is not a world of circumstance, but one meticulously built. Every star carefully placed, every cosmic cycle planned. Every abyssal surge with its contingency, every annihilation accounted for. What architected this work, and for what purpose, is unknowable and ultimately unimportant. When everything was in put in its place, the rules and their exceptions established, seemed to be content to simply watch.

## The Heavens

In the highest reaches, the world began. From a grand, central cosmic engine streamed searing power. Light, heat, and, somehow, condensated of matter, introduced and diffused throughout the world. About this engine trailed innumerable stars, each fulfilling its own purpose in the cosmic clockwork. Far below, on the endless seas, they sparkled, their perfect cycles repeating.

Such careful clockwork, however, demands careful maintenance. Parts are replaced without pause, the new replacing the discarded old in fluid motion. The old, then, is left to fall to the sea. Occasionally, entire decommisioned stars crash down in a relatively cataclysmic event. But, on the scale of forever, such events mean little.

The heavens operate in cycles. Or, rather, their operation dictates cycles of the world. Cycles of years and seasons, days and weeks, millenia and seconds, all imposed by heavenly order.

### Engine of the Cosmos

_A young man watched the sky as the blinding sun cooled into a dull bluish glow. He tracked the now-visible stars, twinkling as they orbited the cooling centerpiece of the sky. They were perfectly in time, as always. If he wanted, he could measure their angles to locate himself exactly in time and space. After years of practice, he could do so by eye. Like his ancestors, long before any clock, calendar, or compass, he marveled at the cosmic timekeepers._

_He stood alone in quiet awe, until his wristwatch called to him. The beginning of his shift. His janitorial duties made for mindless work, leaving him plenty of time to steal glances of the stars through the skylights._

The cosmic engine sits central and unmoving in the sky. By day, it radiates light and life onto the world below. By night, its vestige dims and the world cools. Acting as both sun and moon is a taxing existence, and all of the heavens work to maintain its constant operation.

Naturally, the engine sits atop the immutable cosmic hierarchy. It's birth marked the beginning of the world, and its death would certainly mean the end, if such a thing is possible.

### Servitors in the Skies

Of all the cosmic entities, servitors are the most numerous. Relatively small, and difficult to discern with the naked eye, they float about and tend to the larger machines. This puts them at the bottom of the hierarchy, but each is as important as the engine itself. Should even one be a moment late or an inch out of place, the sky itself could come undone. But that could never happen, at least as so long the laws of reality reign.

Servitors, tracing across the skies in subtle glints, follow much more complex patterns than the larger stars. Their lives are also much shorter, leading to more frequent replacement, and as such, more frequent descents of discarded servitors. While small by cosmic standards, they still make quite a splash. They also have a tendency to still be somewhat operational, often coming to conflict with the primordials it intrudes on.

### Infallible Engineers

Directing the servitors are the engineers, one rung above on the hierarchy of the heavens. They are responsible for the continuous construction that keeps the cosmos in perfect order. They hold blueprints for every star, the specifications of every cycle, and the orders for every servitors.

Most of the smaller stars are engineers. They always circle larger stars, which are often engineers themselves. They create a network of fractal orbits around the central engine. While far less numerous than the servitors, they make up the vast majority of visible stars.

While uncommon, it is possible for a decommisioned engineer to survive its trip through the atmosphere and its subsequent collision with the world below. These survivors, out of place and beyond their warranty, will continue their work to the best of their abilities. This can cause problems, as the seas are a poor place to construct new stars. Fortunately, there is no shortage of titanic primordials who specialize in the violent deconstruction of these cosmic visitors.

### Wardens

_“Power to engines increased. Barrier integrity failing.”_
_Higher, faster, and further than ever before._
_“Power to engines increased. Life support systems offline.”_
_Ribs cracked and visions blurred; the edge of the cosmos loomed._
_“Total system failure. Velocity dropping. Detection of incoming-”_
_The flash left only vapors. The wardens could not allow this intrusion._

The wardens are relatively simple, being terminal in their purpose. They do not build, do not organize, and not necessarily operate on the cosmic cycle. This bold exception to heavenly order exists for good reason, for they maintain the sanctity of heaven itself. They watch the world below, waiting sentinel. Should something make itself a threat to the order of the heavens, the wardens will enact a correction. These impassionate acts of heavenly violence are exceptionally rare, as there are few things in the world that can threaten heaven, either intentionally or incidentally.

Compared to even the engineers, the wardens are very few. Some of most most prominent wardens can be seen even during the light of day, onlly faintly but ever-imposing. Like with the engine, the maintenance of wardens happens in pieces. A full replacement and simultaneous starfall would be a cataclysmic event.

## Named Celestial Bodies

Humans, excercicsing their incessant need to label, have given names to some of the most notable cosmic entities. The most visible cosmic entities have historically served as guides across the seas. They are often more reliable than modern navigation and timekeeping technologies, given the day remains clear and cloudless.

## Astida

The most prominent star in the sky, without contest, and one of the only stars that can be seen during daytime. It circles the sun, orbiting on a seven day period. Over the course of its weekly cycle, erupting in its regular radiance at every new beginning. It begins and ends its cycle pointing north. Or, rather, that orientation has defined north.

## Caeda

A large, pulsing star that sits stationary outside the orbit of Astida to the east. While it can be seen faintly at certain points during the day, it requires special instruments or an especially astute observer to make out the pulses. These regular pulses have a period of exactly one twenty-fourth of a day, with the star brightest at the beginning of each new hour.

## Suna

A hue-shifting chromatic star permanently situated far to the west of the cosmic engine, opposide Caeda but at the very edge of the sky. Dimmer than its eartern counterpart and only visible during the night, it slowly shifts its hues through the visible spectrum, completing a full cycle from red to violet and back every ten years. While not as practically useful as the other named bodies, its vibrant glow has enraptured stargazers since humanity's dawn.

## Twin Sentinels Nada and Kada

Nada, the smaller of the twin stars, orbits Suna at a rather extreme velocity. It completes a full rotation every twenty-four hours. Its passage by its stationary sister marks a new period, and a new day. Small and somewhat faint, it can only be seen during the night. Though one of the smallest wardens, it watches the seas just as vigilantly as its larger siblings.

The larger of the two twin stars, Kada sits stationary to the west of Suna, marking the edge of the sky. Beyond this point, only darkness rests above the seas. While far from the largest warden, it can be easily seen during the day due to its distance from the sun, given one is not too far east.

Humanity has seen both of the twins enact minor corrections. It's unknown what exactly these strikes were correcting, as those far seas are quiet and empty.

## Lyrda

The same distance from the center as Kada, Lyrda is tied for the cosmic entity furthest from the center. It’s a small, dim star that orbits slowly, barely visible on the night sky’s horizon line. It completes its cycle once every one thousand years. Beyond its path lie only gold, glassy oceans that stretch on into oblivion.

## Metora

An otherwise unremarkable star that patrols the inner cosmos in a tight path around the sun. The only reason this warden is recognized among the contless others is that, aside from the twin stars, it is the only warden to have been activated in human history. While witnesses from the event are long dead, the records agree unanimously that this was a very bad time for everybody basically everywhere.

## Zenda, the Wanderer

A strange star with a seemingly erratic behavior that has baffled astronomers for thousands of years. It moves in irregular patterns, meandering across the sky at wildly varying speeds. Not once in all of human history has any sort of cycle been observed, but surely it must have one? Even the wardens, in their reactive nature, are not permitted to just wander through the heavens.
